# Safety Data Scorecard
## Ibuprofen (400 mg)/Tramadol (37.5 mg) Fixed-Dose Combination vs. Monotherapies vs. Placebo — Phase III Dental Surgery Trials

Prepared to support the safety section of a CTD Clinical Overview (Module 2.5). Sponsor for all identified trials: **Farmalider S.A.** (Alcobendas, Madrid). Scope: Phase III, randomized, double-blind trials of the IBU/TRA 400/37.5 mg fixed-dose combination (FDC) specifically in **dental surgery (third-molar extraction) pain**, vs. ibuprofen monotherapy, tramadol monotherapy, and placebo.

## 4. Statistical significance of safety differences

- **Overall AE incidence, IBU/TRA 400/37.5 mg vs. IBU/TRA 400/75 mg**: no statistically significant difference (p = 0.491); the lower-dose FDC nonetheless showed a numerically lower AE frequency (23.5% vs. 42.1%), supporting the lower tramadol dose from a safety standpoint ([PMC12566731, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12566731/)).
- The published paper does **not** report a formal statistical test for AE incidence between the FDC 400/37.5 mg arm and tramadol monotherapy or placebo; it states only that "no clinically relevant differences were identified in the incidence of AEs between treatment groups" ([PMC12566731, Section 3.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12566731/)).
- 1 SAE occurred across the whole study (1.4% of 72 patients), assessed by investigators as **not related** to study medication; no deaths were reported ([PMC12566731](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12566731/)).
- Efficacy-safety trade-off: rescue-medication use (a safety-relevant surrogate for inadequate analgesia) was significantly lower with both FDCs vs. placebo (chi-squared test, exact p not disclosed in the abstract/results excerpt available) — FDC 37.5 mg 35.3%, FDC 75 mg 36.8%, tramadol 52.6%, placebo 94.1% ([PMC12566731, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12566731/)).
- For the two **oral** dental-surgery trials (2013-004637-33 and 2024-517807-35-00), no AE-related statistical comparisons are publicly available — this is a data gap, not a finding of "no difference."
- For context only (not dental surgery, excluded from the table above): in Farmalider's oral hysterectomy-pain Phase III trial, the patent states there were "no differences in the number of adverse effects" between the FDC and placebo arms, and that the tramadol 50 mg arm reported roughly twice the number of AEs of the FDC arm — but no numeric data, population, or statistical test are given, and this is a different surgical population ([EP 3996699 B1, paragraphs 0175–0176](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/f0/53/63/86f8d3032ab542/EP3996699B1.pdf)).

## 5. Data gaps and limitations — important for the clinical overview

1. **The pivotal oral dental-surgery trial (EudraCT 2013-004637-33, n=383) has no public AE data.** The EU Clinical Trials Register entry has no posted results, and the only related document found — patent EP 3996699 B1 — reports only rescue-medication rates (Table 4: FDC 4.2%, ibuprofen 6.2%, tramadol 38.8%, placebo 55.9%) and efficacy (PID) data (Table 3), with **no TEAE, SAE, discontinuation, or AE-cluster figures** for this specific trial. A qualitative AE safety statement in the same patent (favoring the FDC over tramadol) belongs to a *different* trial (oral hysterectomy pain, Example 3) and should **not** be attributed to the dental-surgery trial.
2. **The newest oral dental-surgery trial (EU-CT 2024-517807-35-00, n=346, "Completed")** lists "Adverse events" only as a secondary-outcome category on the registry aggregator; no counts, rates, or comparative statistics have been posted.
3. A related, seemingly earlier version of the oral pivotal protocol (EudraCT 2020-005530-15, "FMLD-IOTRA2-47_FIII") is registered but shows status "Restarted" with no enrollment or results data — its relationship to trial 2013-004637-33 and 2024-517807-35-00 is not fully clear from public sources.
4. **Only the IV pilot trial (2018-001412-30, n=72) has complete, peer-reviewed, MedDRA-coded AE data**, published in September 2025. This is a small dose-finding pilot, not the pivotal oral trial, so extrapolating its safety profile to the oral formulation/dose regimen should be done cautiously in the clinical overview.
5. If your organization holds the Clinical Study Reports (CSRs) for EudraCT 2013-004637-33 and/or EU-CT 2024-517807-35-00, those would be the appropriate primary source for the TEAE/SAE/discontinuation/GI/neuro-cluster data needed to complete this scorecard for the oral dental-surgery trials — the public registries and patent literature do not contain this information.
