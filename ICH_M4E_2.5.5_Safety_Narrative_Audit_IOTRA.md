# Structured Safety Narrative Audit — ICH M4E Section 2.5.5
## Ibuprofen (arginine)/Tramadol Hydrochloride 400/37.5 mg Granules for Oral Solution
### Procedure ES/H/0568/01/DC — Sponsor: Laboratorios Farmalider S.A.

*Draft-ready briefing prepared to map the clinical safety database of the ibuprofen (arginine)/tramadol HCl fixed-dose combination (FDC) against the five core ICH M4E Section 2.5.5 subsections (extent of exposure, common adverse events, serious adverse events, laboratory assessments, and vital sign findings). Source documents cited throughout are the individual integrated clinical study reports (CSRs) and the RMS Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report for procedure ES/H/0568/01/DC; no external hyperlinks are available for these internal regulatory documents, and citation is therefore made by protocol code, version and date in accordance with standard CTD referencing convention.*

The clinical safety database supporting this FDC rests on three studies conducted under the FMLD-IOTRA-20 development programme: study FIIIA (protocol FMLD-IOTRA-20_FIIIA, EudraCT 2013-004637-33), a Phase III trial in an acute dental-extraction (nociceptive) pain model with a single-dose and multiple-dose (24-hour) treatment period, conducted at eight Spanish investigational sites between 4 August 2014 and 25 March 2015 (Informe Final, Version 1.0, 15 November 2015); study FIIIB, a Phase III trial in a post-abdominal/gynaecological surgery (visceral) pain model with a 48-hour treatment period; and study FI (protocol FMLD-IOTRA-20_FI, EudraCT 2013-000196-32), a Phase I randomised, open-label, single-dose, two-treatment, two-period, two-sequence crossover bioavailability/bioequivalence trial in healthy fasted volunteers, conducted at the Clinical Trials Unit of Hospital Universitario de la Princesa, Madrid, between 19 June and 15 July 2013 (Final Report, Version 1, 29 October 2013). Cross-study synthesis is additionally drawn from the RMS Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report for ES/H/0568/01/DC (dated 31 October 2018), which consolidates safety findings across all three studies for the Rapporteur Member State (Spain).

## 1. Extent of Exposure

In study FIIIA, 473 subjects were screened and 384 were randomised and treated (168 men and 216 women; 383 of the 384 constituted the evaluable safety/efficacy population). During the single-dose treatment period, 96 subjects received the FDC (Ibuprofen [arginine]/Tramadol HCl), 97 received Ibuprofen (arginine) alone, 98 received Tramadol HCl alone, and 93 received placebo. Of the 93 placebo-phase subjects, 32 subsequently received the FDC, 31 received Ibuprofen (arginine), and 30 received Tramadol HCl during the multiple-dose extension period, so that every randomised subject ultimately received active study medication as either primary or rescue treatment (FIIIA CSR, Section 12.1, p.134). All 384 treated subjects completed the study. Comparator medicinal products used were Espidifen® 400 mg (Zambon S.A.U.) for Ibuprofen (arginine) and Adolonta® 100 mg/ml (Grünenthal Pharma S.A.) for Tramadol.

Cumulative dosing intensity, summarised across the somatic (FIIIA) and visceral (FIIIB) pain models in the Day 70 assessment report, showed a mean of 6.9 administered doses for the FDC group (median 8.0, SD 2.5, N=92), 5.7 for Ibuprofen (median 8.0, SD 3.0, N=97), 5.5 for Tramadol (median 8.0, SD 3.1, N=99) and 4.5 for placebo (median 3.0, SD 3.2, N=96), reflecting the expected pattern of earlier rescue-medication use in the placebo arm (Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, p.89). The most frequent reason for discontinuing study medication across all groups was the pre-specified 24-hour rescue-medication rule rather than an adverse reaction, occurring in 64.7% of FDC subjects, 64.9% of Ibuprofen subjects, 66.7% of Tramadol subjects and 81.1% of placebo subjects who stopped treatment; other documented reasons included insufficient analgesia, poor tolerability, dizziness, nausea/vomiting, paralytic ileus, diarrhoea, fever, severe headache, patient preference, and protocol-specified exclusions such as concomitant SSRI use (Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, p.90).

In study FI, 24 healthy adult volunteers (17 men, 7 women suitable for pharmacokinetic analysis) received a single oral dose of the FDC and, after a wash-out period of at least seven days, a single co-administered dose of the separate reference products (Espidifen® 400 mg plus Adolonta® 100 mg/ml) in a two-period, two-sequence crossover design; all 24 subjects were included in the safety analysis population and completed the study without premature withdrawal (FI CSR, Section 12.1, p.61).

Study FIIIB followed a comparable single-plus-multiple-dose exposure design in the same four treatment arms over a 48-hour post-surgical observation period; the RMS assessment report references pooled dosing tables spanning both Phase III studies but does not provide a study-specific subject-disposition table independent of FIIIA, and the FIIIB-specific CSR was not available within the document set reviewed for this audit — this represents a residual gap that should be closed with the FIIIB Informe Final prior to finalising the Module 2.5.5 narrative.

## 2. Common Adverse Events

In study FIIIA, 155 of the 384 treated subjects (40.4%) reported a total of 266 adverse events (AEs) during the study, all of which resolved and none of which was assessed as serious (FIIIA CSR, Section 12.2, p.134; independently corroborated in the FIIIA CSR Resumen, p.7). Of these, 193 events were assigned a causal relationship to study treatment by degree of certainty (definite, probable, possible or conditional): 89 were attributed to the FDC, 11 to Ibuprofen (arginine) alone, and 93 to Tramadol HCl alone (FIIIA CSR, Section 12.2.2, p.138). The individual-diagnosis breakdown in Table 42 of the FIIIA CSR shows dizziness, nausea and vomiting as the most frequently reported treatment-related events, concentrated principally in the Tramadol-alone and FDC arms — for example, vomiting was recorded with definite causality in 18 Tramadol-alone subjects and 9 FDC subjects, nausea with definite causality in 6 Tramadol-alone and 7 FDC subjects, and dizziness with definite causality in 11 Tramadol-alone and 9 FDC subjects — a pattern consistent with the known gastrointestinal and central nervous system tolerability profile of the tramadol component. Less frequent related events included somnolence, hyperhidrosis, cold sweat, headache, constipation, dyspepsia, and isolated instances of hyperacusis, hypertransaminasaemia, leucopenia and urticaria, none of which recurred at a rate suggesting a distinct new safety signal for the FDC beyond its individual components.

The Day 70 assessment report's cross-study System Organ Class (SOC) tabulation situates these findings within the broader pooled dataset: gastrointestinal disorders were the leading SOC (52 events, 13.8%, driven principally by nausea [17 events, 4.4%] and vomiting [13 events, 3.4%]), followed by general disorders/administration-site conditions (27 events, 7.0%, largely pyrexia at 20 events, 5.2%), nervous system disorders (22 events, 5.7%, comprising dizziness [10 events, 2.6%] and headache [11 events, 2.9%]), and blood/lymphatic disorders (19 events, 4.9%, principally anaemia, 18 events, 4.7%, attributable to the post-surgical FIIIB population). Across a pooled subset of 209 AE instances, intensity was mild in 67.9%, moderate in 26.3% and severe in 5.7%; causality was assessed as not related in 67.0%, unknown in 18.2%, possible in 10.5% and probable in 4.3%; and outcome was resolution in 89.0%, with 6.2% not resolved, 3.8% unknown, 0.5% resolved with sequelae, and one fatality (0.5%) occurring in the FIIIB study population and discussed under Section 3 below (Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, pp.90–94).

In study FI, 11 of the 24 volunteers (45.8%) reported a total of 16 AEs, individually listed in Table 20 of the FI CSR with onset/resolution dates, severity and causality: five events were judged possibly related to treatment (two following the reference formulations, three following the FDC), while the remainder were assessed as unlikely or unrelated. All were mild or moderate in intensity (the most frequent being headache, reported in six separate episodes across five volunteers, together with single episodes of dizziness, nausea and vomiting, abdominal pain, Bartholin's cyst, common cold, dysmenorrhoea, myalgia and toothache), self-limiting, and none led to withdrawal from the study (FI CSR, Section 12.2.1, pp.61–62).

## 3. Serious Adverse Events

Study FIIIA recorded no deaths, no serious adverse events (SAEs), and no other clinically significant adverse events over the full study duration: "there had not been deaths, other serious adverse events or any other significant adverse events during the study FMLD-IOTRA-20_FIIIA" (FIIIA CSR, Section 12.3, p.138, confirmed again in the Safety Conclusions, p.160, and independently corroborated in the Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, p.95).

Study FIIIB, by contrast, contributed the entirety of the programme's SAE burden. Per the Day 70 assessment report, 12.4% of all AEs in FIIIB were classified as serious, none of which was assessed as related to study medication, while 14.8% of AEs overall were assessed as possibly or probably treatment-related. The cumulative SAE summary (Table 47 of the underlying assessment) recorded 26 SAEs in total, distributed as 4 in the FDC arm, 2 in the Ibuprofen-alone arm, 10 in the Tramadol-alone arm, and 10 in the placebo arm, spanning SOCs including infections and infestations, injury/poisoning/procedural complications, neoplasms (including endometrial adenocarcinoma, glioblastoma, endometrial sarcoma, cervix carcinoma and ovarian tumour, predominantly in older surgical patients), surgical/medical procedures, blood/lymphatic disorders, reproductive system disorders, gastrointestinal disorders, renal/urinary disorders and general disorders; the assessor explicitly requested further characterisation of these 26 SAEs (Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, pp.95–97). One death occurred in the FIIIB Tramadol-alone arm: a 65-year-old female (subject ES05-0019_054, 2 days' exposure to Tramadol 50 mg) developed post-surgical fever, was hospitalised, and was subsequently diagnosed with glioblastoma, dying approximately two months later; the cause of death was attributed to glioblastoma with comorbid peripheral vascular disease, moderate COPD, colonic polyps, adrenal adenoma and a history of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and was assessed as unrelated to study treatment (Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, Table 49, p.97). Eleven FIIIB subjects, all female, discontinued study medication owing to an adverse event, of whom two discontinuations were SAE-related (post-operative wound haemorrhage; vaginal cuff abscess) and nine were related to non-serious events (dizziness; combined nausea/vomiting/headache/fever; pain with nausea; fever managed with intravenous paracetamol; isolated vomiting; dizziness with nausea; abdominal-wall haematoma with post-surgical anaemia; nausea/vomiting; and a constellation of weakness, dizziness, palpitations and blurred vision; diarrhoea) (Day 70 Preliminary Clinical Assessment Report, Table 48, pp.97–98).

In study FI, no serious or life-threatening adverse events were reported, and no AE led to premature discontinuation; the CSR states unambiguously that "none of the adverse events was considered [to have a] relationship with study treatment" that would meet seriousness criteria (FI CSR, Section 12.3, p.62).

## 4. Laboratory Assessments

In study FIIIA, all abnormal laboratory findings were reviewed by the investigational team and judged clinically non-significant with three specific exceptions, each of which was classified as an adverse event and followed to resolution: subject #P122 presented hypertransaminasaemia at the follow-up visit (05 February 2015; ALT/GPT 119 IU/L, AST/GOT 72 IU/L), which normalised on repeat central-laboratory testing (UNILABS) eight days later (13 February 2015; ALT/GPT 74 IU/L, AST/GOT 31 IU/L); subject #P138 presented hyperkalaemia at follow-up (03 February 2015; 5.7 mEq/L), which normalised on repeat testing six days later (09 February 2015; 4.9 mEq/L); and subject #P143 presented hypoglycaemia at follow-up (09 February 2015; 46 mg/dL), which normalised on repeat testing four days later (13 February 2015; 97 mg/dL) (FIIIA CSR, Section 12.4, p.138). No clinically relevant abnormality in physical examination, vital signs or analytical parameters was detected at the screening visit for any of the 384 randomised subjects (FIIIA CSR Resumen, p.8).

The FIIIB-specific laboratory dataset was not independently available within the document set reviewed for this audit; the Day 70 assessment report does not provide a FIIIB-specific laboratory-findings narrative beyond the AE/SAE tabulations already described in Section 3, and the FIIIB CSR should be consulted directly to complete this subsection with study-specific granularity.

In study FI, no clinically significant analytical alteration occurred at either the screening or the follow-up assessment in any of the 24 subjects across either treatment period: "clinically significant analytical alterations did not occur during the study, neither in the screening nor in the follow-up analyses performed" (FI CSR, Section 12.4, p.62).

## 5. Vital Sign Findings

In study FIIIA, no significant alterations in physical examination or vital signs were identified at either the baseline (Visit 1) or the follow-up visit (Visit 3), assessed via Tables 43 and 44 of the CSR (FIIIA CSR, Section 12.5, p.139). At the follow-up visit, aggregate supine vital-sign values across all 384 subjects fell entirely within normal physiological ranges: mean systolic blood pressure was 119 mmHg (SD 12; range 84–166 mmHg), mean diastolic blood pressure was 68.9 mmHg (SD 8.6; range 43–95 mmHg), and mean heart rate was 74.3 beats/minute (SD 12.5; range 46–116 beats/minute) (FIIIA CSR, Table 44, p.160). No pregnancy test was positive among the female subjects tested.

FIIIB employed a comparable physical-examination-plus-vital-signs assessment schedule per its protocol methodology, but study-specific quantitative vital-sign results were not located within the Day 70 assessment report or the document set reviewed for this audit; as with the laboratory dataset, this should be sourced directly from the FIIIB CSR to complete the subsection.

In study FI, vital signs (systolic and diastolic blood pressure, heart rate and axillary temperature) together with 12-lead electrocardiography were assessed at screening and throughout both treatment periods; no clinically significant alteration in physical examination, vital signs or ECG parameters was observed at any timepoint: "clinically significant alterations in the physical exploration, vital signs, or ECG were not found during the study" (FI CSR, Section 12.5, p.63).

## Overall Safety Conclusion (Synthesis)

Across the three studies comprising the ibuprofen (arginine)/tramadol HCl clinical development programme, the fixed-dose combination did not reveal a distinct safety signal beyond that expected from its individual components. Study FIIIA (384 subjects, acute dental pain) reported a substantial burden of non-serious, self-limited AEs (155/384 subjects, 266 events) dominated by the known gastrointestinal and central nervous system tolerability profile of tramadol, with no deaths, SAEs, or clinically significant laboratory or vital-sign abnormalities beyond three transient, self-resolving laboratory findings. Study FIIIB (post-surgical visceral pain) contributed the programme's entire SAE burden — 26 SAEs and one death — but all SAEs and the single fatality were assessed as unrelated to study treatment and were more plausibly attributable to the underlying surgical/oncological comorbidities of an older, post-operative population; nonetheless, the FIIIB CSR should be obtained directly to supply the study-specific laboratory and vital-sign detail not captured in the RMS assessment report, and the assessor's explicit request for further SAE characterisation should be addressed in the final Module 2.5.5 text. Study FI (24 healthy volunteers) demonstrated a clean safety and tolerability profile essentially indistinguishable from the separately administered reference products, with no serious events, no clinically significant laboratory or ECG findings, and a low-grade AE profile (16 events in 11 volunteers, chiefly headache) consistent with single-dose exposure in a healthy population. Taken together, the data support a benefit-risk characterisation of the FDC as comparable in safety and tolerability to its individually administered reference components, subject to closing the identified FIIIB-specific data gaps noted above.
